# A2 → B1: what reliable evidence says, and the design rules it gives our course

Status: research note, 2026-11-10, branch `agent/a2b1-evidence`. Feeds `A2B1_COURSE_BLUEPRINT.md` (PR #1186) and
`COURSE_DESIGN_STATIONS.md` §6 (10 rules added from this file). Owner request 2026-11-10: „найди актуальный материал по
составлению курса от A2 до B1, чтобы он был максимально эффективным, и опирайся только на достоверные источники”.
Russian summary: `docs/review/a2b1-evidence-ru.md`.

**Source policy.** Only official bodies (Council of Europe, Riigi Teataja, Harno, Integratsiooni Sihtasutus, EKI),
peer-reviewed journals (Language Learning, SSLA, MLJ, Applied Linguistics, LTR, CMLR) and recognised handbooks. No
blogs or SEO pages. Labels: **[read]** = primary text or publisher abstract read on 2026-11-10; **[sec]** = finding
confirmed only through a reputable secondary summary (author's own summary, ERIC, OASIS, university repository);
**[not re-checked]** = standard citation known to the author but not re-opened this session — do not quote numbers
from it without checking. **[INF]** = our design inference, not a finding. Effect sizes are Cohen's *d* unless
stated (0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large; Plonsky & Oswald's L2 benchmarks are stricter — treat 0.6 as medium).

**Confidence**: *high* = several meta-analyses or an official norm agree; *medium* = one meta-analysis or several
consistent studies, or effects that depend strongly on conditions; *low* = single studies, theory, expert consensus.

## 1. Official frame (what B1 means for our learners)

### 1.1 CEFR Companion Volume (Council of Europe 2018 / 2020)
Source: *CEFR Companion Volume with New Descriptors* (2018), Appendix 2, Table 2 „Self-assessment grid (with online
interaction and mediation)”, pp. 167–170 — same table in the 2020 edition
(https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4). **[read]**
(the table; the full scales of ch. 3–4 could not be opened: the CoE server returned 403 to the fetcher.)

B1 cells that our blueprint does **not** yet target explicitly:

| Area | B1 self-assessment statement (abridged, CoE 2018 p. 168/170) | In blueprint? |
|---|---|---|
| Written and online interaction | interact about experiences, events, impressions and feelings *if I can prepare*; ask for or give simple clarifications, respond to comments and questions in some detail; interact with a group on a project when visuals (images, statistics, graphs) clarify | partly (family chat note in M12) — **no rule** |
| Mediating a text | convey information given in clear, well-structured informational texts on familiar or current topics | **no** |
| Mediating concepts | help define a task in basic terms; invite others to clarify reasons; ask questions to check understanding and repeat back part of what was said | partly (I-3 repair) |
| Mediating communication | support a shared communication culture (introduce people, exchange information on priorities, ask for confirmation); communicate the main sense of what is said on subjects of personal interest, if speakers articulate clearly and I can pause to plan | **no** |

Action-oriented approach (CV ch. 2, **[not re-checked]** wording): learners as social agents who complete tasks with a
real purpose in a real context; scenario = a chain of tasks towards one outcome. Our module „final task” and v3
stations already follow this. Confidence: *high* (official norm, also adopted by the Estonian curriculum below).

### 1.2 Estonian national curriculum — eesti keel teise keelena
Source: Põhikooli riiklik õppekava, Lisa 1 „Keel ja kirjandus” in the wording of VV määrus nr 18, 23.02.2023,
§ 2.4 (https://www.riigiteataja.ee/aktilisa/1250/6202/5011/18m_pohi_lisa1.pdf). **[read]** (Riigi Teataja shows a
consolidated text valid to 31.08.2026; check whether a newer redaction applies from 01.09.2026.)

- Targets: **A2 by end of grade 3, B1 by end of grade 6, B2 by end of grade 9** („eakohane”). Topic domains: *mina
  ja teised; kodu ja lähiümbrus; Eesti ja maailm; igapäevaelu; õppimine ja töö*, each revisited spirally. Lessons in
  Estonian; authentic texts; active methods; learners directed to Estonian media and events outside school.
- Grade 6 (B1) outcomes include: copes in everyday situations; understands short notices, messages, instructions,
  letters, clear media texts, the gist of an age-appropriate film; finds information in print and web dictionaries;
  **„vahendab raamatu või filmi põhisisu”** (mediation); **„vajaduse korral lihtsustab sõnumit või sõnastab oma
  teksti ümber”** (mediation strategy); writes and fills *kutse, sõnum, e-kiri, avaldus, ankeet*; pronounces clearly.
- Grade 9 (B2) adds: follows discussions, takes notes from talks, argues with pro/contra, writes *avaldus,
  motivatsioonikiri, CV, seletuskiri*, reviews.
- Assessment: based on CEFR level descriptions; errors are a natural part of learning, corrected according to the
  task's aim (§ 1.6).

**Consequence for the 9th-grade track (Milana):** the curriculum expects **B2** by the end of grade 9; the exam
(B1 + B2 tasks, ≥ 60 points for the B1 certificate, per blueprint §8) rewards B2 work. Our course ends at B1, so the
9th-grade track needs explicit B2 tasks (note-taking from a talk, pro/contra argument, *motivatsioonikiri*) — see §4.

### 1.3 Harno B1 exam
Taken from the blueprint ([H], re-read 2026-11-10 by the blueprint agent): 4 parts × 25 points, pass ≥ 60 and no part
at 0. Not repeated here.

### 1.4 Integratsiooni Sihtasutus (adult Estonian courses)
- *Hea tava täiskasvanutele eesti keele õpetamisel* (https://integratsioon.ee/hea-tava-taiskasvanutele-eesti-keele-opetamisel,
  approved by the riigikeele nõukoda). **[read]** Principles relevant to us: language as a means to communication;
  grammar tasks woven into communicative tasks; „Parandame vigu valikuliselt, kaudselt ja toetaval moel”; teach
  through Estonian; „we do not teach to the level exam” but develop ability step by step; explain that becoming an
  independent user takes **about two years on average**; send learners to Estonian media, language cafés, events;
  link tasks to the learner's own life and culture.
- Course volume: the Foundation states its courses total **500 academic hours guided + 180 independent**
  (https://integratsioon.ee/eesti-keele-kursused) **[read]**; the page does not break this down per level (A2 → B1
  hours could not be verified). Registered private B1 programmes found in search list 120–180 contact hours + 50–100
  independent hours (e.g. 150 + 50, 180 + 60, 120 + 80) — market reference only, not a norm. **[sec]**
- [INF] Our 68–90 contact hours (blueprint §2.4) are **below** typical Estonian B1 programmes. It works only with the
  daily practice and real-life use the blueprint already requires; promise learners with little contact a longer
  route (the Foundation's „about two years” is the honest frame).

## 2. Research findings and the rule each gives

| # | Principle | What the evidence says | Conf. | Design rule for our course |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Task-based, action-oriented lessons** | Bryfonski & McKay 2019, *TBLT implementation and evaluation: a meta-analysis*, LTR 23(5) 603–632, 52 studies of whole TBLT programmes: **d = 0.93** on a range of outcomes **[read]**. Integratsiooni SA *hea tava* and the national curriculum prescribe the same. | high | Keep: every module ends in a real-life outcome; every station has a purpose line (already gates `st-final`, `st-purpose`). |
| E2 | **Four strands in balance** | Nation 2007, *The four strands*, Innovation in Language Learning and Teaching 1(1) 2–13: meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning, fluency development, roughly equal time each; Nation calls equal time an arbitrary but useful rule **[read]**. | medium (principled, not an effect size) | Each **module** gives each strand 20–30 % of its minutes; the teacher page tags every station with its strand. |
| E3 | **Fluency strand needs repetition under time pressure** | de Jong & Perfetti 2011, *Fluency training in the ESL classroom*, Language Learning 61(2) 533–568: 4/3/2 with the **same** topic gave larger fluency gains on a new-topic post-test than three different topics; gains rest on proceduralised structures, not repeated words **[sec]**. Bygate's task-repetition studies: fluency rises most consistently, complexity / accuracy mixed **[sec]**. | medium | One fluency station (8 min) in ≥ 2 lessons per module: **fully known** content, time pressure, retold to a **new addressee** (voice message to a colleague after telling the teacher; 2 min → 1.5 min). In 1-to-1 4/3/2 has no new listener, so the change of addressee and channel is what makes the repeat real. **Conflicts with gate `st-repeat`** → owner decision (§4). |
| E4 | **Explicit focus on form inside meaningful work** | Norris & Ortega 2000, Language Learning 50, 417–528, 49 studies: focused instruction gives large gains; **explicit > implicit**; focus on form and focus on forms equally large **[read, abstract]**. Spada & Tomita 2010, Language Learning 60(2) 263–308: explicit instruction effective for simple and complex features **[not re-checked]**. | high | Each teaching lesson: one short notice → rule → practice station (≤ 10 min) for the module's I/P item, then the item is **needed** in the final task. No paradigm table without use (blueprint already says so). |
| E5 | **Corrective feedback: prompts first** | Lyster & Saito 2010, SSLA 32(2) 265–302, 15 classroom studies, N = 827: CF effective and durable; between-group **prompts d = 0.83 vs recasts d = 0.53**; within-group 1.14 vs 0.70; larger in free production; prompts possibly more effective for younger learners **[read]**. Li 2010, Language Learning 60(2) 309–365: overall **d = 0.61**, maintained over time; explicit larger short-term, implicit better maintained; lab > classroom **[read, abstract]**. Hea tava: correct selectively, indirectly, supportively. | high | Teacher page lists **2–3 feedback targets** per lesson (the module's I/P items). During accuracy stations: **prompt first** (clarification request, repetition of the error, elicitation, metalinguistic clue), recast only if the learner cannot self-repair; teens: prompts by default. During fluency and the final task: no interruption, **delayed** feedback on ≤ 3 points in the last 3 min. Writing: focused feedback on the same targets. |
| E6 | **Spaced retrieval for vocabulary and forms** | Kim & Webb 2022, Language Learning 72(1) 269–319, 98 effect sizes, N = 3,411: spacing has a **medium-to-large** effect; longer spacing beats shorter on delayed tests; equal = expanding **[read]**. Mawson & Kang 2025 (classroom meta, 31 effects): distributed > massed **d = 0.54** **[read, abstract]**. Webb, Yanagisawa & Uchihara 2020, MLJ 104(4) 715–738: on average only ~25–40 % of words survive 1–2 weeks after one activity; **flashcards** lead on delayed meaning recall (73 %) vs writing (32 %), gap-fill (24 %) **[read, author summary]**. Butowska-Buczyńska et al. 2024, PNAS 121: retrieval with **varied** context sentences beats constant sentences, more so when spaced **[read, abstract]**. | high | Homework „10 min” = **retrieval**, not re-reading: SRS cards (meaning → form and form → meaning) + sentence practice. **≥ 4 days a week**, short sessions beat one long one. Each active item is retrieved in ≥ 3 sessions spread over ≥ 2 weeks; cards show a **different** example sentence each time. Words met once in class are not „learned”. |
| E7 | **Interleave grammar practice** | Nakata & Suzuki 2019, MLJ 103(3), *Mixing grammar exercises facilitates long-term retention*: interleaved practice > blocked on the 1-week test; lower-scoring learners gained most **[read, abstract excerpt]**. | medium (one strong study + general cognitive evidence) | Module check and home sentence practice **mix** ≥ 3 grammar items incl. ≥ 1 from ≥ 2 modules back; a blocked set only on the day an item is introduced. |
| E8 | **Vocabulary size and text coverage** | Nation 2006, CMLR 63(1) 59–81: 98 % coverage → 8–9k families for written, 6–7k for spoken English **[read]**. Schmitt, Jiang & Grabe 2011, MLJ 95(1) 26–43, N = 661: comprehension rises **linearly** with coverage, no threshold; 98 % reasonable for unassisted reading **[read]**. Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski 2010: 95 % = minimal, 98 % = optimal **[not re-checked]**. No comparable coverage study for Estonian was found; English word families do not transfer to a language with 14 cases. | medium (for Estonian: low on the numbers) | [INF] Every station text: **≥ 95 % of running words known** (A2 base + course items so far + EKI põhisõnavara up to the level) and the rest glossed; exam-format texts may go to 90 % (exam realism). Receptive goal by the end: the EKI learner core (Keeleõppija Sõnaveeb A2–B1); active goal ≈ 850 course items (blueprint). A coverage check script against Ekilex data is a follow-up. |
| E9 | **Recycling in new contexts** | Spacing (E6) plus Uchihara, Webb & Yanagisawa 2019 (Language Learning 69): more encounters → more incidental learning, moderate correlation **[not re-checked]**; varied contexts help (E6, PNAS 2024). | medium | **≥ 60 % of the previous two modules' active items reappear** in the module's texts, cards or tasks — in a new context, not the same sentence (the gate `kordused` already forbids identical sentences). Existing ≥ 30 % per lesson stays. |
| E10 | **Listening: teach the process** | Vandergrift & Tafaghodtari 2010, Language Learning 60(2) 470–497, N = 106, a semester of metacognitive cycles (predict → listen → verify → listen → reflect): experimental group outperformed control; **less-skilled listeners gained most** **[sec]**. | medium | Every listening station: predict (30 s) → first listen for gist → compare / verify → second listen for detail → 1 line „what helped me / what I missed”. Plus one 2-min decoding task a module (endings, quantity, numbers) — the typical L3 losses. |
| E11 | **Pronunciation: comprehensibility first** | Lee, Jang & Plonsky 2015, Applied Linguistics 36(3) 345–366, 86 studies: pronunciation instruction **d = 0.89 / 0.80** **[read, ERIC]**. Saito & Plonsky 2019, Language Learning 69(3) 652–708, 77 studies: explicit teaching effective; larger on controlled, specific measures; prioritise what affects comprehensibility **[sec]**. Meister & Meister 2012 (Interspeech): Russian-L1 speakers separate Q1/Q2 but **not Q2/Q3** in perception or production; Russian has no contrastive duration **[sec, abstract]**. | medium | A 3–5-min perception-then-production micro-task in ≥ 2 lessons a module, on features that change meaning for Russian speakers: quantity (*linna / linna*, *saada / saata*), õ ~ ö ~ ü, first-syllable stress, long vowels in endings. Not on accent. |
| E12 | **Motivation and self-regulation (teens and tired adults)** | National curriculum § 2.4.1: interest and achievability keep motivation; active methods; learning skills and independent work. Hea tava: intrinsic motivation, learner's own life. Self-determination theory (Ryan & Deci 2000, Am. Psychologist 55) — autonomy, competence, relatedness **[not re-checked]**. | low–medium for L2-specific effects | Final task has a learner choice (2 contexts, one teen); visible „can-do” progress per module; homework includes one real-world use (blueprint „10 + 1”). |
| E13 | **Practice frequency** | No L2 meta-analysis fixes minutes per day; distributed practice (E6) says many short sessions > few long. | low (for the exact number) | [INF] 10 min, ≥ 4 days a week, is a floor set by adult time budgets, not a research optimum. Track it (`studentWords`, practice log) and test it in the pilot (blueprint §6). |

**1-to-1 specifically.** We found **no** meta-analysis on one-to-one language teaching; providers' guidance
(British Council, Cambridge) is practitioner advice, not evidence, so it is not cited. The findings above come mostly
from classrooms; CF research shows **larger** effects in dyadic / lab settings (Li 2010), which favours 1-to-1. Risk
specific to 1-to-1: no peer to talk to, so interaction = learner ↔ teacher in a role (blueprint already requires
this), and fluency practice needs a new addressee (E3).

## 4. Changes needed in the blueprint (proposed, for the owner / blueprint agent)

1. **Add mediation** to §3.1 (a column „Mediation task”) — one per module. Examples: M1 explain the registration
   form to a newly arrived relative; M3 pass on the ühistu notice to a neighbour who missed it; M6 tell a friend which
   dish fits her allergy from the menu; M7 tell the family how to take a medicine from the leaflet; M8 explain to a
   parent what the teacher's Stuudium message asks (Estonian → Russian-speaking parent, answer in Estonian to the
   teacher); M10 relay a changed train time from an announcement; M11 explain a phishing warning to a grandparent;
   M12 relay the storm warning to a neighbour; M13 summarise what the neighbour and the board each want; M15 explain a
   course offer to a friend; M16 retell a news item to someone who did not see it; M17 explain an official letter in
   simple words; M18 retell a film clip. Cross-language mediation (a Russian source, an Estonian output) is allowed
   when it is what a real person would do — never sentence translation.
2. **Add online interaction** as a recurring station type (threaded message with a reply to someone else's post,
   clarification request, response to a comment) — ≥ 1 per module.
3. **Add a fluency strand**: ≥ 2 fluency stations a module (E3). This needs an **owner decision**: relax gate
   `st-repeat` for stations tagged `strand: 'fluency'` when the repeat is shorter, to a new addressee or channel,
   and on fully known content. Without it the course has no fluency strand in 1-to-1.
4. **Teacher page: feedback targets** (2–3 forms, prompt-first protocol, delayed feedback after fluency / final
   tasks) — E5.
5. **Listening cycle** in every listening station and one decoding micro-task per module — E10.
6. **Pronunciation micro-tasks** for Russian-L1 priorities — E11.
7. **Vocabulary**: build level lists from EKI Ekilex data (CC BY 4.0, attribution); add a 95 % coverage check for
   station texts; raise module recycling to ≥ 60 % of the previous two modules' items — E8, E9.
8. **Homework**: define „10 min” as spaced retrieval (SRS + mixed sentence practice), ≥ 4 days a week, varied
   example sentences — E6, E7.
9. **Time expectations** (§2.4): state that 68–90 contact hours are below typical Estonian B1 programmes
   (120–180 h contact) and that the Foundation's guidance is „about two years” to an independent user — so the
   course relies on the daily practice and real use; pilot data must confirm it.
10. **9th-grade track**: add B2 tasks required by the curriculum for grade 9 (note-taking from a talk, pro/contra
    argument, *motivatsioonikiri*, review) and check the current PRÕK redaction (from 01.09.2026) before Mock 1.
