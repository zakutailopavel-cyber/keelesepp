# Harno Estonian level exams A2 / B1 / B2 / C1: a checklist for auditing worksheets

Researched 2026-10-12. Everything below is paraphrased. No passages are copied.

**Labels**
- **[H]** confirmed from Harno's official exam page, https://harno.ee/eesti-keele-tasemeeksamid, read in full on 2026-10-12. The page has a separate section for each level.
- **[HB]** confirmed from an official level handbook published on harno.ee:
  - A2: Ilves, *Algaja keelekasutaja* (2008);
  - B1/B2: Hausenberg et al., *Iseseisev keelekasutaja* (2008);
  - C1: Kerge, *Vilunud keelekasutaja* (2008).
- **[KV]** confirmed from Harno consultation booklets (konsultatsioonivihik), which contain real sample tasks.
- **[KS]** confirmed from the Language Act, Annex 1 (Keeleseaduse lisa 1).
- **[INF]** my inference for worksheet design. It is not stated in any official source.

**Rules that apply to every level [H]**
- The exam covers 4 skills, each worth 25% (A2: 20 points of 80).
- Pass mark: 60% of the total, and no skill may score 0.
- The written part (writing, listening, reading) comes first, then the oral part.
- Speaking is done in pairs and recorded. Two raters score it independently.
- Exam topics (teemaringid) are **the same 15 broad topics at every level**:
  - isikuandmed;
  - maja ja kodu;
  - ümbruskond;
  - igapäevaelu;
  - vaba aeg ja meelelahutus;
  - reisimine;
  - suhted teiste inimestega;
  - tervis ja kehahooldus;
  - haridus;
  - sisseostude tegemine;
  - söök ja jook;
  - teenused;
  - kohad;
  - keel;
  - ilm.
- Situations come from 4 domains: personal life, public life, work, education.
- So a level is set by text length and type, the domain, the task demand and the language quality. The topic does not set the level.

## A2 — „Algaja keelekasutaja”

### 1. Exam structure [H]

| Skill | Time | Points | Tasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing | 30 min | 20 | 2 |
| Listening | 30 min | 20 | 4 |
| Reading | 50 min | 20 | 5 |
| Speaking | ~15 min | 20 | 2 |

**Writing**
- T1: information transfer. The learner writes about a person or an institution using the data on a business card, answering 4 bullet questions: who, where, when, how to contact. At least **25 words** [H][KV].
- T2: one of two options, at least **30 words** [H]:
  - a short message or invitation, for example "you found your friend's wallet: where, what it looks like, how they get it back" [KV];
  - a description on a given topic, for example "My weekend", with 3 bullet prompts [KV].

**Listening.** Every text is played **twice**.
- T1: 7 multiple-choice items, 3 options (pictures, numbers or words). Short monologues of about 20–30 s: announcements, instructions, short descriptions, sport commentary.
- T2: 6 true/false items. A dialogue of about 2 min (radio or TV, a simple report).
- T3: 6 short-answer items, **1–2 words** each. A monologue of 1–1.5 min (announcements, instructions).
  - Any answer with the right content counts. Grammar and spelling errors are ignored if the meaning is clear [H].
  - The booklet answer key accepts, for example, „4, neli” or „pannkook / pannkooke” [KV].
- T4: 6 matching items. A dialogue of 2–2.5 min on personal-life topics.

**Reading**
- T1: 6 multiple-choice items, 3 options: everyday set phrases and politeness formulas (etiquette).
- T2: match sentences to 3 short texts of about **60 words** each (newspaper, ads, notices).
- T3: 5 matching items: short notices or a list of places matched to information or descriptions. Sources: signs, packaging, warnings, instructions, ads.
- T4: 5 multiple-choice items, 3 options. A text of about **150 words** (magazine, newspaper, textbook, internet).
- T5: 8 multiple-choice gaps, 3 options each, in a personal letter of about **100 words**.

**Speaking**
- Opens with a get-to-know-you conversation: introduce yourself and answer a few questions.
- T1 (6–7 min): each learner describes **one picture**. The two pictures share a topic but differ (people, events, places, activities). The examiner then asks questions from a topic sheet.
- T2 (5–6 min): an **idea card** with keywords and pictures. One learner asks questions on the topic and the other answers, then they swap roles. A new card follows with a different topic.

### 2. Text lengths [H]
- Reading: about 60, 100 and 150 words. The longest A2 reading text is about **150 words**.
- Listening: 20–30 s clips, up to a 2.5-min dialogue.
- Writing: at least 25 and at least 30 words (minimums, not targets).
- Speaking: about 15 min in total for a pair.

### 3. Topics
- The 15 shared topics [H].
- The handbook adds that A2 texts are mostly **personal-sphere** texts. Public, work and education texts are handled only in a basic way [HB].

### 4. Can-do (paraphrased) [H][HB]
- **Listening**
  - Understands clear, slow phrases on familiar life areas: family, shopping, home area, work.
  - Gets the main point of short, predictable messages and simple instructions, and numbers and facts.
  - Follows the topic of a slow conversation and some of its details.
- **Reading**
  - Understands short simple texts made of frequent and international words.
  - Finds predictable facts in everyday texts: lists, signs, notices, timetables, menus.
  - Understands short personal letters and simple instructions.
- **Speaking**
  - Speaks simply and in general terms on everyday topics.
  - Links sentences with ja / aga / sest / et, or does not link them at all.
  - Takes part in short, direct exchanges of information but cannot keep a conversation going.
  - Halting speech and a clear accent are expected.
- **Writing**
  - Writes simple phrases and sentences linked with ja / ning / aga / sest / et.
  - Writes about family, living conditions, education and work.
  - Writes short stories about daily life, short messages and very simple personal letters.
  - Fills in personal-data forms.
  - Asks for and gives everyday information.

### 5. Grammar and vocabulary [HB]

**Vocabulary**
- An orienting size of **about 2,000 words**. The book's appendix "Esmane sõnastik" (about 2,000 entries) is guidance, not a mandatory list.
- The learner has a narrow everyday vocabulary and makes frequent word and form errors.

**Morphology.** The A2 description says the learner has met, or can form, all of the following:
- **all 14 cases in singular and plural**, including a few short illatives;
- cases in their main functions;
- adjective agreement;
- comparative and analytic superlative;
- ordinal numbers;
- the indicative in **all tenses** (present, simple past, perfect, pluperfect), affirmative and negative;
- the **impersonal in present and simple past**;
- the **conditional present**;
- the imperative;
- nud/tud participles and some v/tav participles;
- ma- and da-infinitive rules;
- **mas / mast / mata** forms of frequent verbs and the **des-form**.

Errors with all of these are frequent, especially in past tenses, negation, infinitive choice and stem changes. The learner "has an overview" of the main structures but uses them with frequent errors.

**Syntax**
- Mostly simple sentences in a row and few complex sentences [HB].
- Three main sentence types: the normal SVO sentence, existential sentences (Tartus on…) and possessor/experiencer sentences (Mul on…).
- Word order with multi-word predicates is hard.
- Polite requests (Kas te paneks…?) and all imperative and prohibition uses are within reach.

### 6. Too easy or below level
- No official source names "too easy" A2 tasks.
- [INF] A2 already expects connected short texts of at least 25–30 words, picture description and asking questions. A2 output should not be limited to single words, except in short-answer listening, where 1–2 words is exactly the official format.

## B1 — „Iseseisev keelekasutaja” (B-level, lower part)

### 1. Exam structure [H]

| Skill | Time | Points | Raw score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing | 35 min | 25 | 20 (T1 = 8, T2 = 12) |
| Listening | 30–35 min | 25 | 30 items |
| Reading | 50 min | 25 | 33 items |
| Speaking | ~15 min | 25 | 20 |

**Writing**
- T1: one of two options:
  - fill in a **questionnaire of 10 questions**, for example customer feedback for a water park. Some questions need a reason ("Miks?") [KV];
  - write a **note** (short message, invitation or card) of about **50 words**, covering 4 points: why, when, who replaces you, how to contact you [KV].
- T2: one of two options, about **100 words**:
  - a **story** on a given title (creative writing);
  - a **personal letter**, with 4–5 bullet prompts [H][KV].

**Listening.** Every text is played **twice**.
- T1: 7 multiple-choice items, 3 picture or word options. Clips of about 20–30 s (monologue or dialogue): announcements, conversations, news, entertainment, sport.
- T2: 6 multiple-choice items, 3 options. A **monologue of 2–3 min** (news, radio or TV).
- T3: 8 items, one of two formats, on a **dialogue or monologue of 3–3.5 min**. Content is scored and minor form errors are ignored.
  - short answers of 1–2 words;
  - gapped sentences completed with a word or number.
- T4: 9 true/false items. An **interview of 2.5–3 min** on public-life topics.

**Reading**
- T1: 9 situation descriptions matched to 6 ads or notices. One ad can fit several situations.
- T2: 6 multiple-choice items, 3 options. A newspaper or magazine text of **200–300 words**.
- T3: 10 multiple-choice gaps (one word, 3 options) in a text of **up to 200 words** (magazine, newspaper, fiction, popular science).
- T4: 8 gaps filled from a **bank of phrases or clause parts**, with **more options than gaps**. Text of **120–150 words**.
- The overview table also allows a word-bank gap task as an alternative to the multiple-choice gap task.

**Speaking**
- Opens with a get-to-know-you conversation.
- T1 (6–7 min), in two parts:
  - (a) the examiner asks questions from a topic sheet and the learners answer in turn;
  - (b) the two learners must **reach an agreement or decision** in a given situation. They state opinions and wishes, describe options and decide, supported by an idea card with pictures. The task counts as done well only if they **reach agreement**.
- T2 (4–5 min): **phone role-play** with a question card and an answer card. One learner calls an institution and the other plays an employee. They ask for and give information.

### 2. Text lengths [H][KV]
- Reading: up to 200 words, 120–150 words and **200–300 words** (longest).
- Listening: 20 s clips up to 3.5 min.
- Writing: about 50 words (or 10 questionnaire answers) plus about 100 words.
- Speaking: about 15 min per pair.

### 3. Topics [H]
- The same 15 topics.
- Text sources widen: news programmes, interviews on public life, newspaper and magazine articles, fiction excerpts, popular science.

### 4. Can-do (paraphrased) [H][HB]
- **Listening**
  - Gets the main point and key details of regular everyday topics (work, school, leisure) in clear standard speech.
  - Follows longer speech if it is slower than normal.
  - Understands radio news and routine phone calls on familiar topics, and detailed instructions.
- **Reading**
  - Understands straightforward factual texts in their field of interest: articles, brochures, manuals, simple fiction.
  - Finds the main idea and specific information in longer texts on familiar topics.
  - Details of unfamiliar topics may remain unclear.
- **Speaking**
  - Copes with most practical situations: asking for information or an explanation, giving an opinion, making proposals.
  - Gives fairly fluent but general descriptions.
  - Describes, compares and analyses even unusual events in simple language.
  - Agrees and disagrees politely.
  - Expresses emotions (surprise, joy, sadness, interest, indifference) and reacts to them.
  - Starts, keeps up and ends a face-to-face conversation without preparation.
  - Long conversations and abstract topics are hard. Speech is a sequence of simple sentences, with pauses.
- **Writing**
  - Writes simple general texts on familiar topics as a linear sequence of short sentences.
  - Writes personal letters and notes, and passes on news.
  - Writes very short standard reports with facts or reasons for actions.
  - Describes a real or imagined event and experiences, in detail on familiar topics.
  - Many errors and clear L1 interference are expected.

### 5. Grammar and vocabulary [HB]
- **Vocabulary**
  - Enough for everyday topics (family, hobbies, work, travel, current events), with occasional circumlocution.
  - Guesses unknown words from context in familiar fields.
  - Uses the most frequent constructions with some variation.
- **Grammar**
  - Understands the meanings and functions of the basic structures.
  - Uses standard patterns fairly correctly to describe events and situations and to express wishes and opinions.
  - Sentence structure and cohesion are simple.
  - Errors are common in spontaneous speech and longer writing but do not block understanding.
- **Points the teaching chapter tags B1/B2 (9.3.2)**
  - agreement scope in the noun phrase;
  - subject–verb dependence;
  - the form of quantity phrases;
  - correct use of function words (adpositions, auxiliaries);
  - referring back to entities in earlier sentences.

### 6. Too easy or below level
- The B handbook says teaching should **not focus on simple linguistic form**. Memorising tables and paradigms without a usage context is ineffective. Form is learned in order to express a function [HB, 9.3.2].
- [INF] At B1:
  - single-word gaps occur only inside a 200-word **text**, with 3 plausible options;
  - banks hold **phrases or clauses with distractors**, not one obvious word per gap;
  - productive tasks need about 100 words with a reason or story line;
  - speaking needs negotiation toward a decision, not just answering questions.
- [INF] A sheet whose closed items can be solved from one visible word, or whose writing is a few sentences without a reason or opinion, is A2-type.

## B2 — „Iseseisev keelekasutaja” (B-level, upper part)

### 1. Exam structure [H]

| Skill | Time | Weight | Tasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing | 80 min | 25% | 2 |
| Listening | 35–40 min | 25% | 4 |
| Reading | 70 min | 25% | 4 |
| Speaking | ~20 min | 25% | intro + 2 |

**Writing**
- T1: a **semi-formal or informal letter**, about **140 words**.
  - Examples: an explanatory note to your boss (what the task was, why it failed, how to prevent it next time), or an email recommending a job opening to a friend [KV].
  - Scored on: task fulfilment (relevance, structure, layout, length), the addressee, the right **register**, vocabulary range and precision, grammar, varied sentences, natural linking, punctuation.
- T2: one of two options, about **180 words**:
  - a **summary based on source data, with your own comment**;
  - an **argumentative text** with pros and cons and a concluding position [KV].
  - Scored on: generalising from data and drawing conclusions, stating and justifying main points, **supporting them with examples**, logic, cohesion, style.

**Listening**
- T1: 5 multiple-choice items, 3 options. Authentic clips of **35–45 s** (announcements, speeches, lectures, news, debates). **Played once.**
- T2: 5 multiple-choice items. An authentic interview or talk show of **3–4 min**, played twice.
- T3: 10 gapped sentences, filled with **1–2 words**, from a **3–4 min** lecture, talk or review. A misspelled answer counts as wrong if the meaning is obscured.
- T4: **12 true/false** items. An interview with 2 interviewees or a talk show with 3 speakers, 3–4 min, played twice.

**Reading**
- T1: 7 multiple-choice items, 3 options. An authentic opinion, problem or review article of **500–600 words**.
- T2: one of two formats:
  - 8 statements, tips or warnings matched to 6 paragraphs of a **300–400-word** text (handbooks, institutional websites, manuals);
  - 8 short answers of **1–3 words** to a **200–300-word** informational text.
- T3: 12 multiple-choice gaps, **4 options**, word **or expression**, in a text of about **250 words**.
- T4: 8 gaps filled with **whole sentences** from a bank with **one extra sentence**. Text of **350–400 words**.

**Speaking**
- Opens with a 1-min conversation about the learner's work or studies.
- T1 (7–8 min in total): each learner gives a **1-minute monologue**: a speech at a meeting, a short talk or a presentation on a **work-related** topic.
  - A topic card describes the situation and the task. **2 min preparation**, notes allowed.
  - The partner then asks one relevant question. The card can be swapped once.
- T2 (9–10 min, discussion ≤ 4 min): discussion, debate and decision.
  - First, each learner gives an opinion on the examiner's questions, justifies it and comments on the partner's view.
  - Then comes a situation: the pair must agree on a **joint position**, using **argument cards A (pro) / B (con)** plus their own arguments.
- Scored holistically:
  - genre fit and structure of the talk;
  - highlighting what matters;
  - answering questions fluently;
  - pro and con arguments, defending a view with examples;
  - taking and keeping the turn and linking to the partner's turn;
  - even tempo;
  - fairly accurate and varied grammar, and a wide vocabulary.

### 2. Text lengths [H]
- Reading: about 250, 200–300 or 300–400, 350–400 and **500–600 words** (longest).
- Listening: 35–45 s clips; long texts of 3–4 min.
- Writing: about 140 and about 180 words.
- Speaking: a 1-min prepared monologue plus about 4 min of discussion.

### 3. Topics [H]
- The same 15 topics.
- The work domain is explicit in speaking: work-related talks and work meetings.
- Text sources include opinion and problem articles, popular science and travel writing.

### 4. Can-do (paraphrased) [H][HB][KS]
- **Listening**
  - Gets the gist of fairly long, complex standard speech.
  - Follows discussions and complex argument on familiar topics.
  - Understands details of news, interviews, films and lectures.
  - Picks up speakers' views and intentions and the level of formality.
  - Listens critically, draws conclusions and catches sociocultural hints.
- **Reading**
  - Reads long, complex texts for different purposes, using strategies.
  - Quickly grasps the content of longer texts and selects relevant details.
  - Generalises across several texts.
  - Understands **problem articles with differing viewpoints**.
- **Speaking**
  - Gives clear, detailed descriptions.
  - Gives prepared talks.
  - Expands an argument with comments and examples, justifies a view and compares positions.
  - Interacts fluently and spontaneously.
  - Takes the turn and joins discussions.
  - Conveys shades of feeling.
- **Writing**
  - Writes clear, detailed texts, including on familiar abstract topics.
  - Writes reasoned essays or reports with **pros and cons**, and reviews.
  - **Synthesises** information from several sources and summarises it.
  - Adapts style to a known or unknown reader.
  - Uses varied sentence types, including complex sentences and common **non-finite clauses (lauselühendid)**.

### 5. Grammar and vocabulary [HB]
- **Vocabulary**
  - A wide vocabulary in general and own-field topics.
  - Varies expression to avoid repetition.
  - Understands common idioms and expands vocabulary through compounding and derivation.
  - Picks the right level of formality.
- **Grammar**
  - Good command of grammar, with no errors that cause misunderstanding.
  - Links ideas with varied cohesive devices.
  - Uses complex sentences and some non-finite clauses.
  - Corrects many of their own errors.
- **Typical persistent errors:** consonant-gradation forms, **object case choice**, case forms of verbs, tense use, **government (rektsioon)** and word order.
- **Points the teaching chapter tags B2**
  - government structures (rektsioonistruktuurid);
  - paraphrasing sentences through regular derivation (nominalisation);
  - non-finite clauses;
  - emphatic stress and word order by information structure.

### 6. Too easy or below level
- [INF] At B2, every official closed task works on a **long authentic text** of 250–600 words.
  - Gaps choose among 4 words or expressions, or among whole sentences.
  - Matching is statement-to-paragraph.
- [INF] A B2 sheet with single-sentence items, 3-option word gaps or short texts under 250 words is B1-type.
- [INF] Writing without a required register or addressee, or an opinion without pro and con arguments and examples, misses the B2 criteria.

## C1 — „Vilunud keelekasutaja”

### 1. Exam structure [H]

| Skill | Time | Points | Raw score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing | 90 min | 25 | 24 (12 + 12) |
| Listening | 45 min | 25 | 27 |
| Reading | 60 min | 25 | 34 |
| Speaking | ~20 min | 25 | 20 |

**Writing**
- T1: a **generalising summary from source data**: two charts, graphs or tables. About **180 words**.
  - Compare, analyse and draw conclusions. Cite only a few key figures.
  - **No personal opinion** is expected.
  - Neutral, factual tone for a public reader.
- T2: a longer **opinion text**, **220–260 words, not more**.
  - Genre: an opinion piece (formal journalistic) or an opinion statement (semi-formal).
  - Starts from a situation description and **2 points to develop**, and both must be analysed.
  - Clear introduction, development and conclusion, with examples.
  - Must not turn personal. The addressee is an institution or the public.

**Listening**
- Authentic radio, with possible background noise or music.
- T1: 7 open short answers of **1–3 words**. News recordings of 2–3 min, played twice.
- T2: 10 multiple-choice items, 3 options or sentence completions. A **conversation or debate of about 7 min, played once**.
  - Needs **inference**. The last item may concern the whole text.
- T3: **note-taking from a lecture**. A monologue of about 10 min, played once.
  - Afterwards the learner has 15 min to write the **10 main ideas** in 1–2 sentences each.
  - Scored 0–5 for content. Spelling is not scored.

**Reading**
- T1: 12 statements matched to 4 paragraphs of a text of about **600 words** (fiction, opinion or problem article, popular science).
- T2: 10 gaps filled with **sentences** (3 options) in a text of about **550 words**. The sentence must fit both content and form.
- T3: 12 short answers of **1–4 words** to long authentic informational texts (service and product descriptions, manuals). A partly correct answer scores 0.

**Speaking**
- Opens with 1–2 min of introductions and talk about the learner's work.
- T1 (about 12 min in total): a **2-minute talk** on a work-related topic, chosen from 2 on the card. **3 min preparation.**
  - Fixed structure: address, development, short conclusion. Factual, not personal, in the right register.
  - The partner asks 1–2 questions. Reacting to questions weighs more than expert knowledge.
- T2 (about 7 min): **discussion** on a topic card with a few guiding ideas.
  - Turn-taking, keeping and yielding the turn; it must not become a monologue.
  - Opinions are justified with examples.

### 2. Text lengths [H]
- Reading: about **550–600 words** for the long texts, plus long informational texts.
- Listening: 2–3 min, about 7 min and about 10 min.
- Writing: about 180 words and 220–260 words (hard cap).
- Speaking: a 2-min talk plus about 7 min of discussion.

### 3. Topics [H]
- The same 15 topics.
- In practice the content is public and work oriented: the speaking talk is work-related and the writing addresses institutions or the public.

### 4. Can-do (paraphrased) [H][HB][KS]
- **Listening**
  - Follows long speech on abstract, unfamiliar topics, even when its structure is unclear and links are implicit.
  - Understands idioms and register shifts.
  - Follows group debates and most lectures.
  - Gets information from poor-quality announcements.
  - Catches hidden attitudes and speaker relationships, and infers mood and intent from cues.
- **Reading**
  - Understands long, complex texts in detail, inside or outside their field (with re-reading).
  - Catches fine details, including attitudes and unstated nuances.
  - Skims quickly to judge relevance.
- **Speaking**
  - Gives clear, detailed talks on complex topics, links subtopics and ends with a conclusion.
  - Speaks fluently and almost effortlessly, getting round vocabulary gaps.
  - Defends official positions convincingly and answers objections.
  - Uses intonation for nuance, idioms, humour and allusion.
- **Writing**
  - Writes clear, well-structured texts on complex topics.
  - Highlights what matters, supports views with reasons and examples, and concludes.
  - Writes in a personal, natural style aware of the reader.
  - Grammar errors are rare and hard to notice.
  - Layout and punctuation are consistent.

### 5. Grammar and vocabulary [HB]
- **Vocabulary**
  - Rich, with idioms and colloquial expressions.
  - Gets round gaps by circumlocution.
  - Derivational suffixes (-kond, -stik, -la, -lane, -line) help with unknown words.
- **Grammar**
  - Used freely. Agreement is near error-free.
  - A wide range of subordinate clauses.
  - Growing use of **non-finite clauses** (des- and mata-clauses as adverbials and modifiers, extended participles as modifiers and complements, mine-constructions).
- **Still hard at C1**
  - unusual government patterns, e.g. kelle nimel, uhke mille üle, põhinema millel;
  - auxiliary-verb patterns, e.g. pruugib teha, juhtusin nägema;
  - **object case**;
  - phase and change-of-state expressions, e.g. läheb katki, jääb seisma.

### 6. Too easy or below level
- [INF] Official C1 tasks always involve one of the following:
  - synthesis across sources (charts, lectures);
  - inference from long texts heard once;
  - genre and register control with a word cap;
  - sentence-level gaps with form and content constraints.
- [INF] Word-level gaps, single-text comprehension questions with literal answers, or personal-experience writing are below C1.

### Contradictions with official sources

1. **The grammar sequence is later than the A2 handbook.**
   - `GRAMMAR` places these targets later than A2:
     - enneminevik, des-form, plural genitive and conditional at `B1-`;
     - the impersonal and mas/mast/mata at `B1`;
     - plural partitive and täisminevik at `A2+`.
   - The code comment calls later items "too early as a target".
   - The official A2 handbook says an A2 learner has met or can form: all 14 cases in **singular and plural**, all indicative tenses including perfect and pluperfect, the impersonal in present and past, the conditional present, mas/mast/mata and the des-form. Errors are expected [HB].
   - Fix: make it two-tier. A2 = recognition and formation with errors; B1/B2 = accurate, varied use. It should not read as "not before B1".
   - The B handbook marks as B2: non-finite clauses (lauselühendid), government structures and derivational paraphrase. That matches `B2`.
2. **`bank: 'no'` at B1, B1+, B2- and B2.**
   - Harno uses answer banks at B1 (a bank of phrases or clause parts, with more options than gaps) and at B2 (a bank of whole sentences with one extra).
   - The official rule is about **what** the bank holds and that it has distractors, not whether a bank exists.
   - Suggestion: replace `bank` with a bank-unit rule:
     - A2: words;
     - B1: phrases or clauses, options > gaps;
     - B2/C1: sentences plus at least 1 distractor.
3. **C1 writing `[250, 400]` words is too long.**
   - Harno C1 asks for about 180 words (data summary) and **220–260 words, capped** (opinion text).
   - Suggestion: `[180, 260]`.
4. **C1 reading `[500, 900]` has an upper limit above the exam.**
   - The exam uses about 550–600 words.
   - 900 is not official. It is acceptable as stretch material but not as a norm.
5. **C1 and B2 `speaking` ranges do not match the monologue lengths.**
   - B2 monologue = **1 min** after 2 min preparation. C1 talk = **2 min** after 3 min preparation.
   - Discussions are about 4 min (B2) and about 7 min (C1) **in pairs**.
   - levels.js gives B2 180–300 s and C1 240–420 s, which suggests a solo monologue several times longer than the exam's.
   - Suggestion: split the field into `monologue` and `interaction`.
6. **B1 writing `words: [100, 180]` goes above the exam.**
   - Harno B1 = about 50 words plus about 100 words.
   - 180 belongs to B2 (about 180-word argument). For B1 a norm of 50–120 words is closer.
7. **B1 `inference: true` and the B1 `can` line ("järeldab…") overreach.**
   - Officially, inference and critical listening appear at **B2**, and hidden meaning at **C1**.
   - B1 reading and listening target main idea, key details and information search.
   - Keep inference light at B1, e.g. simple cause and effect. This is [INF], but the sources support it.

### Missing in levels.js (present officially)

- **Text-type and genre norms per level**:
  - A2: business card, notice, message, description, personal letter;
  - B1: questionnaire, note, story, personal letter, ads, phone call;
  - B2: (semi-)formal letter or explanatory note, summary with comment, argument, meeting speech;
  - C1: chart summary without opinion, opinion piece, lecture notes, work talk.
- **Register or addressee requirement** from B2 upward (a scoring criterion).
- **Speaking task formats**:
  - A2: picture description, idea-card questions;
  - B1: negotiate to agreement, phone role-play with cards;
  - B2: argument cards pro/con;
  - C1: structured talk and turn-taking.
- **Short-answer length** (1–2 / 1–3 / 1–4 words) and the rule that content counts more than form in A2/B1 listening answers.
- **Matching type by level**: sentence to text (A2), situation to ad (B1), statement to paragraph (B2/C1).
- **Gap-option count**: 3 options at A2/B1, **4** at B2.
- **Number of replays**: 2× at A2/B1; single play enters at B2 (short clips) and C1 (long texts).
- **Domain shift**: personal life at A2; work and public life explicit in B2/C1 speaking and writing.
- **A2 vocabulary size**: about 2,000 words, with "Esmane sõnastik" as the official reference list.
