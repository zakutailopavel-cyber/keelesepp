# Eesti keel C1 (B2 → C1): the 18 modules

Course plan for the new course (owner 2026-10-30, course key `b2c1`, lessons `b2c1-001…090`). The gates, exam map and archetype plan
are in `docs/COURSE_FINAL_SHAPE_C1.md`; this file holds what the course is about. **Plan only: no lesson exists yet**, and titles are
working titles. Content starts after the A2 course is finished. The can-do lines are in Estonian, as they will be on the sheets; each
lesson will be written so that its can-do is exactly what the main task of Kasuta does (V9, V10).

## 1. Principles

- **Harno's 15 topic circles, at C1 depth.** The topics are the same at every level; the level is set by text type, domain, task and
  language. So each circle is taken up as an abstract, professional or public question, not as personal life. The first 15 modules cover
  one circle each; 16–18 integrate.
- **One style axis per module**, not a grammar syllabus. The learner knows the forms. What is hard at C1 (Kerge's handbook, `HARNO_LEVEL_REQUIREMENTS.md`
  C1 §5): non-finite clauses, nominalisation, register, government, object case, phase expressions, hedging, cohesion.
- **Input reaches exam length gradually.** Reading from ≈ 400 words in module 1 to 550–600 by module 4; listening from 2–3 minutes to a
  7-minute debate by module 6 and a 10-minute lecture by module 12 (mock 060); writing from ≈ 150 words to the capped 220–260 by module 5.
- **Never personal.** The story supplies cases, but the learner's W2 and S1 turn a case into a general argument: the exam penalises
  anything else.

## 4. Reuse from the C1 süvakursus

The süvakursus (`est-c1-*`, 10 modules, 100 lessons, `docs/C1_CURRICULUM_240.md`) stays as it is and is a different kind of course: thematic and
grammatical, with separate grammar lessons and progress checks, not exam-shaped. **What may be reused is the choice of topic and its grammar
notes as an idea list (named per module below). No sentence, text, task or item is copied:** V7 and V8 check only within a course,
so this rule is on the author. The two courses have separate ids, baselines, badges and curriculum ids.
